# Employee Attrition Analysis

## Project Overview
This project analyzes employee attrition patterns to identify key factors associated with employee turnover. The analysis aims to provide insights that can help Human Resources (HR) teams understand employee retention challenges and support data-driven decision-making.

## Business Objectives
- Analyze the overall employee attrition rate.
- Identify departments and job roles with higher attrition rates.
- Examine the relationship between overtime and employee attrition.
- Explore the association between job satisfaction, monthly income, and employee turnover.
- Analyze attrition patterns based on employee tenure.

## Dataset
- Dataset: IBM HR Analytics Employee Attrition & Performance
- Total Records: 1,470 employees
- Initial Columns: 35
- Data Cleaning: Removed 3 constant columns.

## Tools & Technologies
- Python
- Pandas
- NumPy
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- Tableau

## Analysis & Key Findings

1. Overall Attrition: 16.12% of employees left the company.
2. Department: Sales had the highest attrition rate at 20.63%.
3. Job Role: Sales Representatives had the highest attrition rate at 39.76%.
4. Overtime: Employees working overtime had a higher attrition rate (30.53%) compared to those who did not (10.44%).
5. Job Satisfaction: Employees with low job satisfaction had an attrition rate of 22.84%.
6. Tenure: Employees with 0–1 years of tenure had the highest attrition rate at 34.88%.
7. Monthly Income: Employees who left had a lower median monthly income (3,202) compared to employees who stayed (5,204).

## Business Recommendations
- Evaluate employee retention strategies in departments with higher attrition rates.
- Review overtime policies and workload distribution.
- Strengthen onboarding and support programs for new employees.
- Evaluate compensation and employee satisfaction.

## Project Limitations
The findings represent associations within the dataset and do not establish causation. The dataset does not provide direct financial information about employee turnover costs.

## Conclusion
This analysis highlights several employee characteristics and workplace factors associated with attrition. The findings can help HR teams identify areas that may require further investigation and develop more informed employee retention strategies.
